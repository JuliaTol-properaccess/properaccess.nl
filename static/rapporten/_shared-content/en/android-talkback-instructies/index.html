To experience the findings in this report for yourself, test the app with TalkBack — the screen reader that comes built-in on every Android phone and tablet. Blind and low-vision users rely on it to operate their device. Below are the steps to turn TalkBack on and the gestures you will need.

Note: two gesture sets. TalkBack has two variants of some gestures. The multi-finger gestures (two, three and four fingers) only work on Android 11 or later, and only on Pixel 3 or newer, Samsung Galaxy, and a handful of other devices. On older or unsupported devices you use the one-finger L-shape alternatives instead. Where the difference matters, we list both below. Want to know which set your device supports? Turn TalkBack on and do a three-finger tap — TalkBack will tell you whether multi-finger gestures are active.

Tip. Spend a few minutes in Practice gestures mode first (on multi-finger devices: four-finger tap; otherwise open the TalkBack menu → Help & feedback). You can try any gesture there without the app reacting to it. TalkBack tells you which gesture you just made.

Turning TalkBack on and off

The fastest way is the volume key shortcut. Go to Settings → Accessibility → TalkBack → TalkBack shortcut and enable it. From then on, turn TalkBack on or off by holding the volume up and volume down keys together for a few seconds.

Alternatives:

  • Settings → Accessibility → TalkBack → toggle on or off.
  • Ask Google Assistant: "Hey Google, turn on TalkBack" or "… turn off TalkBack".

The essential gestures

Once TalkBack is on, your device behaves differently than you are used to. A single tap only selects an element — you have to double-tap to activate it. These are the gestures you need to move around a screen:

Gesture What it does
Swipe right with one finger Jump to the next element.
Swipe left with one finger Jump to the previous element.
Drag one finger across the screen Explore by touch — TalkBack announces every element your finger touches. Useful when you do not know where on the screen something is.
Double-tap with one finger Activate the element TalkBack has selected (open a link, press a button, toggle a checkbox). You do not have to tap on the element itself.
Double-tap and hold with one finger Long press (for context menus and similar actions).
Two-finger tap Pause or resume speech. Handy when you want to take a note.
Two-finger swipe up, down, left or right Scroll the screen.

Opening the TalkBack menu

The TalkBack menu is a built-in menu with commands like Read from top, Find on screen, navigation settings and help. You open it in two ways:

  • L-shape (always works): in one smooth motion, swipe down and then right, or up and then right.
  • Multi-finger (if supported): three-finger tap.

Inside the menu you will find Read from top (read the whole screen from the beginning — essential for checking reading order) and Read from next item (read on from the current element).

Reading controls: jumping by element type

The reading controls are Android's equivalent of the VoiceOver Rotor on iOS. You first pick a type of element to jump through — for example only the headings, only the links, or only the form fields — and then move from one element to the next.

  • Cycle through reading controls: swipe in one L-shape up and then down, or down and then up. On multi-finger devices: three-finger swipe up or down. Repeat the gesture to cycle through the available reading controls. TalkBack announces which setting is active ("Headings", "Links", "Controls", …).
  • Next element of that type: swipe down with one finger.
  • Previous element of that type: swipe up with one finger.

Which reading controls are available is configured under TalkBack settings → Customize menus → Customize reading controls. Some options are off by default.

System navigation with TalkBack

When TalkBack is on, a number of device actions also work as gestures:

  • Back: swipe down and then left.
  • Home screen: swipe up and then left.
  • Recent apps: swipe left and then up.
  • Open notifications: swipe right and then down, or two-finger swipe down from the top.

Reproducing findings

Use this instruction as a reference: if a step refers to "the next heading", you know you have to set the reading controls to Headings and swipe down with one finger.
