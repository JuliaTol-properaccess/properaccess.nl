To experience the findings in this report for yourself, test the app with VoiceOver — the screen reader that comes built-in on every iPhone and iPad. Blind and low-vision users rely on it to operate their device. Below are the steps to turn VoiceOver on and the gestures you will need.

Tip. Spend a few minutes in Gesture Help first: four-finger double-tap. In that mode you can try any gesture without the app reacting to it. VoiceOver tells you which gesture you just made.

Turning VoiceOver on and off

The fastest way is to set up the accessibility shortcut. Go to Settings → Accessibility → Accessibility Shortcut and choose VoiceOver. From then on, turn VoiceOver on or off by triple-clicking the side button (iPhone X or newer) or the Home button (older models).

Alternatives:

  • Settings → Accessibility → VoiceOver → toggle on or off.
  • Ask Siri: "Hey Siri, turn on VoiceOver" or "… turn off VoiceOver".

The essential gestures

Once VoiceOver is on, your device behaves differently than you are used to. A single tap only selects an element — you have to double-tap to activate it. These are the gestures you need to move around a screen:

Gesture What it does
Swipe right with one finger Jump to the next element.
Swipe left with one finger Jump to the previous element.
Double-tap with one finger Activate the element VoiceOver has selected (open a link, press a button, toggle a checkbox). You do not have to tap on the element itself.
Triple-tap with one finger Long press (for context menus and similar actions).
Two-finger swipe up Read the whole screen from the top.
Two-finger swipe down Read from the current element onwards.
Two-finger tap Pause or resume speech. Handy when you want to take a note.
Three-finger swipe up or down Scroll the screen.
Four-finger tap at the top or bottom of the screen Jump to the first or last element on the screen.

The Rotor: jumping by element type

The Rotor is the most important tool for navigating a screen quickly. You first pick a type of element to jump through — for example only the headings, only the buttons, or only the form fields — and then move from one element to the next.

  • Open or change the Rotor: twist two fingers on the screen as if you were turning a physical dial. VoiceOver announces which setting is active ("Headings", "Links", "Form controls", …). Keep turning until you hear the option you want.
  • Next element of that type: swipe down with one finger.
  • Previous element of that type: swipe up with one finger.

Which options are available in the Rotor is configured under Settings → Accessibility → VoiceOver → Rotor.

Reproducing findings

Every finding in this report includes a "How to reproduce" section. It tells you exactly which steps and gestures to use to see the problem for yourself. Use this instruction as a reference: if a step refers to "the next heading", you know you have to set the Rotor to Headings and swipe down with one finger.
