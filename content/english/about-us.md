# Independent web accessibility auditors, based in Amsterdam

Proper Access audits websites and apps for digital accessibility, against WCAG 2.2 and the European standard EN 301 549. We work for companies that serve consumers in the Netherlands and must comply with the [European Accessibility Act](/en/european-accessibility-act/), and for public sector bodies under the Dutch government decree. Our clients are in the Netherlands, Sweden and Germany, and we report in English or Dutch at no extra cost.

## What we are, and what we are not

We audit and advise. We do not build websites, do not manage content and never audit our own work, so there is no conflict of interest in what we report. Our own software, the [WCAG Radar](/en/tools/wcag-radar/), is a checker your team uses in the browser; it is built and hosted in the EU.

## How we work

- **A team of 10**, trained in house and working on accessibility full time. We have been **WAS-certified by the International Association of Accessibility Professionals (IAAP)** since 2023.
- **More than 950 audits** of websites and apps since 2019, for 63 organisations, measured 7 August 2026. Four in ten clients come back for a second audit.
- **The six-eyes principle.** A senior auditor runs the audit, a second auditor does the internal review, and every report is checked by three people before it reaches you.
- **Tested by hand.** With a screen reader (NVDA, VoiceOver, TalkBack), keyboard only, zoom up to 400%, and on real devices for apps.
- **A report per component**, not per WCAG criterion. Everything that is wrong with one component is in one place, with a fix.

## One user story per finding

Every finding in our report starts with a user story, written from the perspective of the visitor with a disability. Here is one from a recent report:

> As a visitor who navigates with a screen reader, a keyboard, a screen magnifier, or who relies on a predictable reading flow, I need dynamically inserted content to appear in the DOM directly next to the element it relates to, because only then does the screen reader announce it in context, does keyboard focus reach it at the expected moment, does my magnified viewport still contain it, and does my attention stay connected to whatever triggered it.

Below that come the technical explanation, the code example and the screenshot. The developer or editor who has to fix it no longer has to guess why it matters. See an [example report](https://audit.properaccess.nl/reports/202503_carolinesvedbom/).

## After the report

Once your report is delivered, you speak to the auditor who wrote it. Questions run through our [accessibility credits](/en/accessibility-credits/): one credit is one question on one topic, and follow-up questions in the same thread are free. Before your report is delivered, a relationship manager is your point of contact.
