sections_header: "What the ILT looks at"
sections:
  - eyebrow: "Scope"
    title: "The digital side of the journey"
    body: "The ILT describes digital accessibility in passenger transport as: a passenger can plan, book and make a journey without difficulty. Concretely it names websites and apps with travel information, digital ticketing that works with a screen reader, and display screens and announcements at stations and in vehicles. The legal basis is Directive 2019/882, the European Accessibility Act, which lists services related to passenger transport by air, bus, rail and water among the services that must be accessible."

    card_icon: "directions_bus"
    card_title: "Who this concerns"
    card_list:

      - "Rail and bus operators with journey planners, apps and e-tickets"
      - "Ferry and cruise operators with online booking"
      - "Booking platforms that sell these services to consumers"
  - eyebrow: "Supervision"
    title: "Reports from passengers, and findings sent to the carrier"
    body: "A passenger who runs into a problem is asked to contact the carrier first. If that does not solve it, the passenger can file a report with the ILT through its online form. The ILT then checks the service and tells the carrier where it does not comply. Services that were already running before 28 June 2025 have a transition period for products that already existed, until 28 June 2030; a website or app you maintain today has no transition period."
    card_eyebrow: "Not the ILT"
    card_icon: "info"
    card_title: "Two things supervised elsewhere"
    card_list:
      - "**Ticket machines and check-in terminals** are products under the EAA; the RDI supervises them. Terminals in use on 28 June 2025 may stay until the end of their economic life, at most twenty years."
      - "**A webshop that sells travel goods** rather than transport falls under the ACM."
  - eyebrow: "What we find"
    title: "Where transport websites and apps break for passengers"
    body: "We test the whole route, from searching a journey to holding a ticket, on the website and in the app. That means the journey planner and its results with a screen reader, the date picker and the seat map with the keyboard alone, the payment step, the time limit on a booking or check-in, and the e-ticket and confirmation documents. Each of those can stop a passenger before the journey starts."

faqs:
  - question: "Does the EAA apply to a foreign airline or ferry operator selling to passengers in the Netherlands?"
    answer: "The directive lists passenger transport services among the services that must be accessible, and the Dutch regulators describe the obligation by the service and by the customer being a consumer. Whether your company, registered elsewhere, is in scope for the Dutch market is a legal question; have it checked. If you sell tickets to consumers in the Netherlands, we would assume it applies until counsel says otherwise."
  - question: "Is the timetable screen at the station part of this?"
    answer: "The ILT names display screens and announcements at stations and in vehicles as part of digital accessibility in passenger transport. We audit websites, apps and documents; for physical displays we can advise, but the audit work we do is on the digital channels."
  - question: "Our e-tickets are PDFs. Do they count?"
    answer: "Yes. An e-ticket is part of the service, and a PDF that is a scanned image or has no reading order cannot be read by a screen reader. We check the ticket and confirmation documents as part of the audit."
  - question: "Where can I read the ILT's own page?"
    answer: "On the ILT page [digital accessibility in transport](https://www.ilent.nl/onderwerpen/passagiersrechten/digitale-toegankelijkheid) (in Dutch), which also holds the reporting form for passengers."
